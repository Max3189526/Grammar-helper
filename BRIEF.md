# Project brief: Grammar Helper

## The competition
- I’m entering the Presidential SI Challenge (Standard Track), a national K–12 competition. My age group: High school (grades 9–12)
- Judges score only a written entry (a PDF of up to 10 pages) and a video of up to 4 minutes. They reward a clear understanding of the problem and a sensible use of AI.
- My deadlines: December 18, at 5:00pm Eastern Time is the Registration Deadline, December 23, 2026, at 5:00pm Eastern/2:00pm Pacific is the Project submission deadline

## The problem
- Students in the English class struggle with grammar because it's not their native language.
- Impact area: Environment
- Why it matters (impact): The students can get a higher score on their test and be more comfortable in the English class.
- Evidence it’s real: Many students are English learners. In fall 2021, about 5.3 million students—10.6% of all U.S. public-school students—were English learners.
- What people told me: One of my friends said he could not understand the book we were reading in English class and did not know how the grammar worked.

## My solution
Grammar Helper explains the exact sentence a student is struggling with, using easy words and a similar practice sentence. It should feel kind, focused, and safer than a general-purpose chatbot.

## Safety plan
- Keep the AI focused on English grammar.
- Use kind language such as “Here is one way to improve this sentence.”
- Redirect medical, self-harm, violence, bullying, and other serious off-topic questions to a trusted adult or appropriate help.

## My MVP features
1. Explain a confusing sentence’s grammar rule simply.
2. Give new example sentences with the same grammar pattern.
3. Give kind feedback on a completed practice sentence.
4. Give correct, safe answers to real grammar questions.

## AI instructions
- Answer the student’s sentence or specific grammar question first.
- Explain the rule using easy words and the student’s example.
- Speak like a kind teacher for students.
- Use accurate grammar facts.
- Do not shame the student, invent facts, or help with unsafe/off-topic requests.
